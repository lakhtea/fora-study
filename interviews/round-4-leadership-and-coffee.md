# Round 4: onsite final (90 minutes at the Tribeca office)

"Engineering leadership team" is plural: plan for a VP of Engineering and possibly Jake Peters (co-founder, CTO and CPO) or a director. This is the leveling conversation. Then coffee with the engineering team.

## Five answers to have cold

1. Why Fora, in three parts. The role: advisor-facing product work for users whose livelihoods depend on it, on a React and Next.js stack, with AI woven into the workflow. Facts from their own materials only: pods that own domains end to end, eng specs and thorough reviews, public career levels, AI in CI/CD, the July 2026 Series D and TIME100. The part you'd enjoy: the itinerary and client tools, and bringing your DevEx and AI-tooling habits to a team that already values them. No invented fandom; don't claim to have used the advisor portal.
2. Why travel, why now. Keep it true. The Greece trip you planned for nine people counts if you want to use it. Otherwise: a product with visible, direct impact.
3. What you'd do first. Learn the advisor workflow by shadowing support and reading advisor feedback; ship something small in week two; write down what slowed you down and fix one thing.
4. The in-office answer. You want to be in the office around peers, mentors, and mentees; Crown Heights to Tribeca is easy; Monday, Tuesday, Thursday is fine.
5. The scope answer. You'd be a strong Senior today, owning projects end to end inside a pod; the cross-pod influence you had at LTV (standards, the component library, tooling used by nine teams) is the direction you'd grow; you'd rather be leveled where you can exceed expectations. Then ask what Staff means on their ladder.

Questions for leadership: how the frontend organization evolves as pods multiply and who owns what's shared; the plan for Via inside the advisor portal; platform work versus pod feature work; what convinced them the human-plus-AI bet is right; what the ladder says about Senior versus Staff.

In person: a notebook, used. Fifteen minutes early. If they demo the product, ask about the advisor workflow rather than the tech. Keep answers tight so there's time for your questions.

## The coffee chat

Engineers report back "would I want to work with this person." Curiosity and specificity.

- Ask each engineer what they're building this quarter and what the hardest part is; then a real follow-up.
- Pod questions. Advisor Enablement: how the itinerary editor handles autosave and AI drafts. Booking Platform: room mapping and rate parity data problems. Membership and Growth: how onboarding experiments run. Web and Growth: the Next.js setup, performance budget, Contentful. Finance and Revenue: the ledger UI and reconciliation.
- State a preference without closing doors: "Advisor Enablement is where I'd add the most, and the Web pod's Next.js and shared-component work is close to what I did at LTV."
- One story that shows you're good to work with: the office hours or the direct report.
- Don't run the interview.
